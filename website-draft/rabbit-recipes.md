# Rabbit on the table: a global recipe and food-history collection

**Intan Isle public-learning draft · 25 November 2026**  
**Status:** public learning page on the Intan Isle GitHub repository; curated sampler, not exhaustive. This is a curated sampler across regions, not an exhaustive survey or a claim that every community eats rabbit.

## A note before you browse

Rabbit is food in some communities and a companion animal in others. Both perspectives deserve respect. All dishes below are cultural and culinary examples, not medical advice or a claim that Intan Isle has launched or tested these products. Historical methods are not food-safety guidance; current local law and safe handling always apply. “Bunny chow” is a South African curry-in-bread dish; the name does not mean it contains rabbit. Roman-era recipes below are for hare, a related but different animal, and are included only as a historical comparison.

The sampler currently spans Europe, North America, South America, Asia, Africa and Oceania. It is source-led but not exhaustive; historical record density and available translations vary. Antarctica has no established human culinary tradition to represent here.

## Fun facts: rabbit through time

- Archaeological evidence from Pié Lombard in southern France reports Neanderthal use of rabbits for meat and pelts during the last interglacial period. It does not show a modern rabbit-farming system.
- Rabbits have long been food and fur animals in parts of Europe, while New World rabbit species followed different histories. A history of rabbits is not one universal domestication story.
- The familiar story that a medieval pope’s decree caused rabbit domestication is not supported by the genetics and historical evidence reviewed by Frantz and colleagues. Avoid repeating it as settled fact.
- Durham University’s medieval research describes rabbit as a high-status English royal food in the 13th century; records include a royal gift of live rabbits and rabbit on a royal feast requisition list. The status and price context changed over time.
- Malta’s traditional food register preserves rabbit stew, fried rabbit and pie, while Mexico’s historic cookbooks document rabbit with chile and coriander. These examples show culinary variety, not universal acceptance.
- “Bunny chow” is not rabbit meat: it is curry served in a hollowed loaf. The name is a common source of confusion.
- The modern Intan Isle concepts—saddle cuts, sausages, nuggets, patties, broths and shepherd’s pie—are founder product-development ideas, not historical claims, validated recipes or products for sale.
